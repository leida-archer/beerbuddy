## Problem Statement

Nevada County, CA residents (Grass Valley, Nevada City, Penn Valley, Truckee, etc.) have to manually check multiple stores' weekly ads or drive store-to-store to figure out where to buy beer most cheaply. Stores in the area span big chains (Raley's, Save Mart, Grocery Outlet, Walmart Grass Valley, BevMo nearby), local favorites (Holiday Market, SPD), and indie liquor stores. Prices change weekly. There is no single place to see "best beer deals near me this week" — and that's the question users actually have on a Friday afternoon.

BeerBuddy is a mobile-first responsive web app (PWA) that surfaces this week's best beer deals near the user, ranked by genuine value (deal vs. typical price) rather than only sticker price.

## What Makes This Cool

- **Local and concrete.** Nevada County is small enough to cover well — every relevant chain plus a handful of indies. The user's friends and neighbors will actually use it. Sierra Nevada Brewing is *literally local* (Chico-adjacent), so beer is a culturally relevant utility, not a generic price-comparison play.
- **The "is this actually a deal?" framing.** Every other beer-price project does "cheapest now." BeerBuddy compares this week's price against a 90-day baseline so a chronic low price (Natty Light @ $19.99 always) doesn't crowd out a real deal (Sierra Nevada Pale Ale @ $13.99, normally $17.99). This is the differentiator.
- **PWA on mobile feels like an app without app-store overhead.** "Add to Home Screen" puts the icon on the user's phone, opens fullscreen, no install friction. One-tap from idle to "where do I drive."

## Constraints

- **Scope: Nevada County + adjacent commute corridor.** Core: stores within ~25 miles of Grass Valley/Nevada City — i.e., the chains Nevada County residents actually drive to. This explicitly includes BevMo Auburn (~20 mi south) since it's a routine destination for beer specifically. **Truckee:** chain adapters that already cover Truckee locations as a side effect (e.g., Save Mart, Safeway) will surface those stores automatically — no extra work. We do **not** separately scrape Truckee-only indies, and Truckee is not part of the v1 success-criteria coverage measurement. Roseville/Auburn beyond BevMo (e.g., Total Wine, Costco Roseville) is post-v1 unless trivially covered by a chain adapter.
- **Solo developer, side-project effort.** No team, no funding, no business pressure. Project must run on free tiers and survive months of low attention.
- **Web app, not native.** Responsive PWA, mobile-primary. No App Store / Play Store submission.
- **Free and ad-free.** No monetization plan in v1. If sustainability becomes a problem, revisit (affiliate links, "tip jar," donations).
- **Legal posture: passive scraping of public web pages and ingestion of publicly distributed weekly ad PDFs only.** No ToS-violating scraping, no fake user accounts, no inventory of personally-identifiable purchases. Indie store prices entered manually by the developer, not crowdsourced anonymously (avoids liability around inaccurate prices on third-party indie stores).

## Premises

The following premises were agreed during the office-hours session:

1. **Web-first is sufficient.** Mobile-first responsive PWA matches the use case ("find a deal before I leave the house"); native is unnecessary overhead.
2. **One county is the right v1 scope.** Nevada County is small enough to cover deeply and ground-truth, large enough to have real users (~100K residents).
3. **"Best deal" beats "cheapest."** Sticker price is shallow; deal-vs-baseline is what users actually want to know. $/oz and ABV-aware sorting available as secondary views.
4. **Sustainability is a real concern.** Solo + statewide + scrapers + free was identified as a recipe for abandonment. Constraining to one county and emphasizing low-maintenance ingestion (LLM circular parsing > brittle HTML scrapers wherever possible) is a deliberate hedge.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Single-chain MVP (Total Wine only)

- **Summary:** Scrape Total Wine product API for the Roseville/Auburn stores. Single-page web app with ZIP input. Ships in a week.
- **Effort:** S — ~1 week human / ~1 day CC
- **Risk:** Low
- **Pros:** Fast validation; low commitment.
- **Cons:** Total Wine is *outside* Nevada County (~30+ mile drive from Grass Valley). Misses every store users actually shop at. Wrong product for the chosen scope.
- **Reuses:** Standard web stack.

### Approach B (chosen): Multi-source Nevada County deal aggregator

- **Summary:** Per-source ingestion adapter pattern covering Raley's, Save Mart, Grocery Outlet, Holiday Market, SPD Markets, Walmart (Grass Valley), and BevMo (Auburn) — chains where weekly digital circulars or product pages are publicly available. LLM-based circular parsing (Haiku) where direct scraping is infeasible. Manual entry for indie liquor stores. Postgres database tracks 90 days of history per (store, product) pair, enabling "deal vs. baseline" detection. PWA web app shows ranked deals near the user's ZIP, with a per-product detail view showing price history and all stores carrying it.
- **Effort:** M — ~2 months human / ~6 weeks part-time with CC (one focused weekend per week; see Next Steps for the per-week breakdown)
- **Risk:** Medium — chain adapters require occasional fixing when stores change their HTML; LLM costs ~pennies per week.
- **Pros:** Covers ~80% of where Nevada County residents actually buy beer; differentiated framing; sustainable on free tiers; each adapter is independent (one breaks, app still works).
- **Cons:** More upfront architecture; needs ongoing maintenance of ~7 adapters; cold-start indie data is manual.
- **Reuses:** Adapter pattern (well-known); Haiku for PDF parsing (one prompt + cheap inference); standard Next.js + Postgres + Vercel free tier stack.

### Approach C: "Beer Drop" — weekly notification feed (lateral)

- **Summary:** Reframe as a weekly email/SMS/RSS push of curated deals in Nevada County. No live search app. Public web feed. One cron job per week, no real-time queries.
- **Effort:** S — ~2 weeks human / ~3 days CC
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Pros:** Most "for fun"-friendly; content engine pulls in audience over time; no live app to babysit.
- **Cons:** Different product than user envisioned; no "I'm out and about, where's the deal" use case.
- **Reuses:** Same ingestion as B without the live query layer.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — Multi-source Nevada County deal aggregator.**

Rationale: it's the actual product the user described, sized correctly to one county (per the premise check), and the AI-compression ratio for adapter-style work (~30x for feature implementation, ~50x for boilerplate) makes the human-2-month estimate tractable as 2–3 weeks of CC-assisted effort. Approach A was correctly rejected because Total Wine is outside the chosen geography. Approach C is genuinely interesting and could be added later as a notification layer on top of B's ingestion — but as a v1 it's a different product than what was envisioned.

### Visual sketch

A 3-screen wireframe (mobile portrait, intentionally rough) is at `/tmp/beerbuddy-sketch.html` (auto-opened in default browser at design time):
- **Screen 1 — Entry:** ZIP input + "use my location"; the empty state lists which chains are covered.
- **Screen 2 — Deal list:** filter chips (pack size, style, sort by $/oz); the best deal sits in a heavier-bordered card; each card shows price, "was" price, store + distance.
- **Screen 3 — Deal detail:** big price, $/oz, 90-day trend graph (the key differentiator visualized), and a list of nearby stores carrying the product with directions.

### Architecture sketch

- **Frontend:** Next.js (App Router) PWA. Tailwind for styling. Deployed on Vercel free tier.
- **Backend:** Next.js API routes for read queries; Postgres on Neon free tier; geo lookups via PostGIS extension or in-process Haversine (Nevada County is small enough).
- **Ingestion (revised 2026-05-04 after web audit):** GitHub Actions cron (one schedule per source) running TypeScript adapters. Each adapter outputs `{store_id, product_id, pack_size, price, observed_at}` rows. **Default ingestion engine is Playwright (headless Chromium), not plain `fetch()`.** The web audit found that nearly every modern chain e-commerce surface (Raley's, Save Mart, BevMo, Holiday Market) is a JavaScript-rendered SPA where simple `fetch` returns an empty body. Plain `fetch` + HTML parsing is reserved for: PDF circulars (Save Mart, Grocery Outlet, Holiday Market all publish them as static PDFs), and simpler chain pages built on traditional server-rendered stacks (SPD's IGA-hosted weekly ad page).

  **Data-surface map (verified 2026-05-04 via direct fetch attempts):**
  | Chain | Primary surface | Render | Verdict |
  |-------|-----------------|--------|---------|
  | Raley's | `raleys.com/store/213` | SPA | Playwright OR DevTools-discovered backend API |
  | Save Mart | `shop.savemart.com` weekly ad collection (Shopify-ish) | SPA | Playwright OR Shopify API endpoint |
  | Save Mart (alt) | weekly-ad PDF if exposed by `savemart.com/flyers/{store_id}` | static PDF | plain fetch + Haiku parse |
  | Grocery Outlet | `groceryoutlet.com` weekly ad (Wed→Tue cycle) | mixed (PDF available) | plain fetch on PDF |
  | BevMo Auburn | `bevmo.com/products/{id}` | SPA, static metadata in HTML, price loaded by JS | Playwright for price; static metadata via fetch |
  | SPD Markets | `spd.iga.com/WeeklyAd/Index/` (IGA partner) | likely SSR | plain fetch |
  | Holiday Market | `shopholidaymarket.com` shop + `holiday-market.com/weekly-ad` | SPA + static ad | Playwright for shop; plain fetch on weekly ad |
  | Walmart Grass Valley | `walmart.com/store/{N}` | SPA, internal API exists | API discovery preferred; Playwright fallback |

  **Day 1 of Week 1 (revised task):** API-discovery sweep across the seven chains. Open DevTools on each chain's site, identify the JSON endpoints that feed the SPA, document them in `/lib/ingest/adapters/{chain}/api-notes.md`. Where a clean API exists, the adapter can skip Playwright and hit the API directly with `fetch` (faster, more reliable, less brittle). Where no clean API is discoverable, the adapter uses Playwright. This task is the most important of Week 1 — it determines per-chain implementation cost.

- **Delivery-aggregator adapters (revised 2026-05-04):** A complementary adapter type covering Instacart, DoorDash, Caviar, and Toast online-ordering pages. These platforms display live per-store pricing for both chain stores AND indie stores that have no scrapeable website of their own. The web audit confirmed this data exists but is **harder to access than chain primaries** — DoorDash returned 403 forbidden via direct fetch (Cloudflare anti-bot); Instacart and Caviar are likely similar SPA + bot-detection surfaces. Path forward: Playwright with realistic browser fingerprinting, possibly with proxy rotation if rate-limited. Recommendation: defer delivery-aggregator adapters to Week 4 or later, after the seven primary chain adapters are working — they're a force multiplier for indie coverage but not blocking for v1.

- **Playwright cost on GH Actions free tier:** Each Playwright run costs ~30–60s of GH Actions minutes vs. ~1–2s for plain fetch. For 7 chains × daily × ~45s = ~5.25 min/day = ~160 min/month. Plus the orchestrator overhead and matview refresh = ~200 min/month total. Free tier is 2000 min/month for private repos (unlimited for public). Plenty of headroom. **If the project ever runs out of free minutes, switching the repo to public is the cheapest fix.**
- **Storage discipline (Neon free tier = 0.5 GB):** store **price-change events**, not daily snapshots. Schema: a row is written only when an adapter observes a price *different* from the prior most-recent row for that `(store_id, product_id, pack_size)`. Daily idempotent runs that see no change write nothing. Estimated steady-state: 7 chains × ~500 SKUs × ~1 change/week = ~3,500 rows/week → ~180k rows/year, well under free-tier limits. If we exceed, prune to 1-year rolling window.
- **LLM circular parsing:** For PDF or screenshot inputs, use Anthropic Haiku with a structured-output prompt. Roughly 1 input/week × 5–7 chains × ~$0.001 = pennies/month.
- **Indie store data (revised 2026-05-04):** **Default path is web-scrape via delivery aggregators** (DoorDash, Caviar, Instacart, Toast online-ordering). Most Nevada County indie liquor and grocery stores have at least one of these surfaces with live prices. The original "single admin-only manual entry form" is preserved as a **fallback for the rare zero-web-presence indie**, but it is no longer the default. Auth on the admin form remains: static password in a Vercel environment variable, checked server-side, IP-based rate limit (5 failed attempts per hour). The admin form is **not promoted in user-facing UI under any circumstance** (see Product Principle: Zero User Labor in DESIGN.md).
- **Product normalization (the riskiest piece):** Each chain has its own SKU naming ("SIERRA NEV PA 12PK CAN" vs. "Sierra Nevada Pale Ale 12-pack 12oz"). v1 strategy: **exact-string-match with a manual override table.** When two chain rows refer to the same product, the developer adds a row to a small `product_aliases` table mapping chain SKUs to a canonical product. LLM-assisted suggestion of likely matches is a v1.5 enhancement, not v1. Budgeted as its own week (see Next Steps).
- **Geo:** US Census ZCTA centroids for ZIP-code-to-lat/lon; OSM Nominatim for store geocoding (cache results).
- **"Best deal" formula (canonical default):** `price_drop ≥ 10%` vs. the rolling 90-day median for that `(product, pack_size)` across all stores in the database. Ranked by absolute dollar savings descending. **Cold-start (first 90 days):** baseline = the longest history we have, with a visible "limited history" caveat in the UI on each affected card. Formula is intentionally simple in v1 and may be revisited after real-world feedback.

## Open Questions

1. **Indie liquor store coverage long-term — mostly answered (2026-05-04).** The /design-consultation web audit found that most Nevada County indies have at least one scrapeable presence on DoorDash, Caviar, Toast online-ordering, or their own Square/Clover-powered shop page. v1 covers indies via these delivery-aggregator adapters; manual admin entry is reserved for the genuine edge case. **Crowdsourcing is explicitly off the table** — see Product Principle: Zero User Labor in DESIGN.md. The remaining open part: monitor which specific indies in the county have no scrapeable surface at all, and decide at month 3 whether to maintain manual entries for them or simply omit them from coverage.
2. **Is the 10% / 90-day-median "best deal" formula right?** It's the canonical default in the Architecture sketch but should be revisited after real-world friend feedback. Possible alternates: rank by % off; weight by `$/oz` improvement; surface "first sale in N weeks" instead of magnitude. Don't tune until there's data + feedback.
3. **Per-chain data surface (validate before adapter contract is locked).** As noted in Architecture, Flipp/WRAPP iframe widgets may force a screenshot+LLM-vision path for some chains. The adapter contract must accommodate both HTML/PDF and screenshot inputs cleanly. Day 1 of Week 1 is reserved for this audit.
4. **Robots.txt and ToS per source.** Each source needs a quick legal check before ingestion is built. Save Mart, Raley's, etc. publish circulars publicly with no clear restriction; chain product pages may have stricter ToS. Document per-source legal posture in each adapter's README.
5. **Sustainability tier vs. cost ceiling.** If steady-state data growth approaches Neon free-tier limits sooner than projected, the choice is between aggressive pruning (drop history older than 1 year) or upgrading to Neon's $19/month tier — which busts the <$10/month sustainability target. Decide *which* sustainability target matters more (cost ceiling or data fidelity) before it becomes urgent.
6. **Production error tracking (Sentry/Logflare).** Captured in /plan-eng-review. Wire a free-tier exception sink before the Week 6 soft-launch goes wide so client/API runtime crashes are surfaced without needing to check GH Actions logs. The `runs` table covers ingestion failures already; this is for everything else.
7. **"Beer Drop" weekly notification feed (Approach C from /office-hours).** Captured in /plan-eng-review. v1.5 candidate. Once v1 ingestion has been stable for ≥30 days, add a weekly Resend-powered email + public RSS feed of top deals. Reuses the existing matview; only new work is subscriber management + send logic. Revisit after Week 8 success-criteria check.
8. **Tap-to-tooltip on the 90-day trend graph.** Captured in /plan-design-review. v1 chose static graph with Y-axis labels (Issue 8A) — sufficient for visceral "going up vs. down" reading. Add tap-to-tooltip if Week-8 friend feedback requests "what was the price on a specific date." ~2hr CC. Depends on /design-consultation (tooltip styling).
9. **Master-detail desktop layout (≥1024px).** Captured in /plan-design-review. v1 chose mobile-on-desktop centered column (Issue 7A) — "secondary web" interpretation. Worth building only if desktop traffic materializes meaningfully. ~6hr CC. Depends on /design-consultation.

### Information architecture (per surface)

**Entry page (`/`):**
1. Wordmark + tagline (context)
2. Question prompt + ZIP input (primary action)
3. "Use my location" link (secondary action)
4. Coverage footer ("Updated weekly from Raley's, Save Mart, …") (reassurance)

**Deal list (`/deals?zip=NNNNN`):**
1. Page header: ZIP + store-count summary
2. *(if cold-start)* Limited-history banner — dismissible, sticky for 24h
3. Sort dropdown: "Sorted by ▾ Best deal" — Best deal / Cheapest absolute / Cheapest $/oz
4. Filter chip row: pack size, style — chips are filter-only, not sort
5. Best deal card: stronger left-edge accent + inline `BEST DEAL · X% off` badge; "Updated 2d ago" relative-time label; *(cold-start)* small "◌" dot indicating limited history for this product
6. Subsequent deal cards: same fields without the BEST DEAL badge or accent

**Deal detail (`/deals/[productSlug]`):**
1. Back affordance
2. Product name (large)
3. Style + ABV + pack meta
4. Big price + $/oz + "Updated X ago"
5. 90-day price-trend SVG: Y-axis shows min, current, and max prices labeled at the right edge; static (no hover/tap interactivity in v1)
6. "Stores carrying this product (N)" header
7. Store cards (cheapest first): name, address, distance, current price, "Directions →" button (deeplinks to Apple/Google Maps via universal URL)
8. Web Share API affordance ("Share this deal") at the bottom of the detail card

**Admin pages (`/admin/indie`, `/admin/aliases`):**
- Workflow-first, no aesthetic ceremony. Single column, ample whitespace, large form controls, password gate above the fold. Indie entry: store dropdown → product autocomplete → price field → submit → toast confirmation + recent-entries list below the form. Aliases: side-by-side candidate-match cards with one-click confirm/reject + 5-second undo affordance.

### Trust & freshness

- Every price displayed in any surface includes a relative-time label: "Updated this morning", "Updated 2d ago", "Updated last Wed".
- Cold-start (first 90 days, or any product with <30 days of history) shows a "◌" dot on cards and a banner on the list page. Removed automatically as data accrues.
- Disclaimer in the page footer: "Prices may vary at point of purchase. Verify before driving."

### Returning-user behavior

If a user has previously entered a ZIP (cookie or localStorage), `/` redirects to `/deals?zip=<lastZip>` automatically. The entry page becomes a one-time first-run experience, not a daily friction point. A "change ZIP" link in the deal-list header allows the user to re-enter.

### Responsive

- **Primary target: 320–480px portrait mobile.** Optimized layout.
- **Tablet 768–1024px:** Same single-column layout, centered, max-width 480px. Effectively "mobile-on-bigger-screen."
- **Desktop ≥1024px:** Same single-column layout, centered, max-width 480px (per /plan-design-review Issue 7A). "Secondary web" interpreted as "works fine on desktop, not optimized for it."

### Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA baseline, locked from day 1)

- All images have alt text; the trend SVG has `<title>` and `<desc>` describing min/max/current; price-trend labels are also rendered as text for screen readers.
- All interactive elements have visible focus rings (2px outline + 2px offset, color TBD by /design-consultation).
- All tappable elements ≥44×44px. Filter chips currently sketch ~28px and need to grow.
- Color contrast: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 (calibrated when /design-consultation picks colors).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: disable any card/banner animations, no spring physics on interactions.
- `prefers-color-scheme`: support OS dark-mode preference.
- Keyboard navigation: tab order matches visual order; ZIP input → submit → use-my-location on entry; sort → filter chips → first card → next card on deal list.

### Known stale artifacts

The original wireframe at `/tmp/beerbuddy-sketch.html` is now superseded by the specs above. It does not show the BEST DEAL badge, the sort/filter separation, the limited-history banner, the "updated X ago" timestamp, the trend Y-axis labels, or the desktop centering. /design-consultation should produce a new round of mockups that incorporates these specs.

## Success Criteria

- **Personal-use threshold (week 4):** developer uses BeerBuddy at least once a week to inform an actual beer purchase.
- **Friend-use threshold (week 8):** at least 3 friends/family in Nevada County use it at least once after a soft share. Feedback collection is intentionally low-tech in v1: verbal/text from a small group, no analytics SDK, no event tracking. Just ask people.
- **Coverage threshold:** all 7 target chains (Raley's, Save Mart, Grocery Outlet, Holiday Market, SPD, Walmart Grass Valley, BevMo Auburn) have working ingestion at completion of Week 4. **Steady-state operating threshold:** at least 6 of 7 actively producing fresh data on any given week (i.e., one chain may be temporarily broken — site changed, scraper out of date — without the project being considered "down"). At least 80% of beer products visible in-store at Raley's Grass Valley appear in the database within 7 days of price change.
- **Sustainability threshold (month 3):** total monthly cost < $10. Maintenance time < 1 hour/week on average. If either is exceeded, see Open Question #5 for the explicit decision.
